## 🌌 The Grand Challenge: Neural Operators and the Illusion of Success

Over the past decade, Scientific Machine Learning (SciML) has witnessed an explosion of neural surrogate models—such as Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs), DeepONets, and Conditioned U-Nets—designed to replace costly numerical partial differential equation (PDE) solvers. By learning direct mappings between initial conditions and future states:

$$u(t) \xrightarrow{\mathcal{S}_\theta} u(t + \Delta t)$$

these neural operators promise speedups of $100\times$ to $10,000\times$ over traditional finite difference, finite volume, or spectral solvers.

In standard academic benchmarks, these surrogates often boast impressive single-step accuracy, achieving relative errors under $1\%$. **However, when deployed in realistic scientific or engineering simulations over long time horizons, they almost universally fail.**

## ⚠️ The Autoregressive Trap: Covariate Shift in Dynamical Systems

To understand why neural PDE surrogates fail, we must examine the geometry of dynamical systems:

### 1. Attractors are Measure-Zero Manifolds
In dissipative non-linear systems (such as the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation or viscous Navier-Stokes), trajectories initialized from random fields rapidly lose energy in high-frequency modes and collapse onto a low-dimensional subset of state space known as the **inertial manifold** or **chaotic attractor** $\mathcal{A} \subset \mathcal{H}$.

When training data is collected passively by simulating trajectories from random initial states, $95\%$ of the gathered transitions $(u_t, u_{t+1})$ lie directly on this attractor $\mathcal{A}$.

### 2. The Compounding Error Mechanism
During inference, the surrogate is applied autoregressively:
$$\hat{u}_{k} = \mathcal{S}_\theta(\hat{u}_{k-1}, \mu), \quad k = 1, \dots, K$$

Even if the one-step error $\varepsilon_k = \hat{u}_k - u_k$ is small, its direction is almost never tangential to the attractor manifold $\mathcal{A}$. Instead, numerical error kicks the state $\hat{u}_k$ slightly **transverse (orthogonal)** to the attractor.

### 3. The Lack of Restoring Forces
Because the surrogate $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ was trained exclusively on the attractor $\mathcal{A}$, it has never observed off-manifold states:
- It has never learned how the physical PDE naturally dissipates off-attractor perturbations back toward $\mathcal{A}$.
- Instead, it misinterprets these small transverse errors as physical features, amplifies them in subsequent steps, and triggers catastrophic divergence (high-frequency spectral explosion or unphysical shock blowup).

## 🛑 Why Classical Active Learning Fails for PDEs

To fix this data distribution problem, the natural machine learning instinct is to apply **Active Learning (AL)**: query the numerical solver on states where the surrogate is most uncertain.

Standard pool-based Active Learning algorithms (such as **Ensemble Variance Sampling**, **BALD**, or **SBAL**) proceed as follows:
1. Simulate a large pool of candidate trajectories from random initial conditions (e.g., $10\times$ oversampling).
2. Score all candidate states using surrogate ensemble disagreement:
   $$\sigma^2(u) = \frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{m=1}^M \| \mathcal{S}_{\theta_m}(u) - \bar{\mathcal{S}}(u) \|^2$$
3. Select the Top-$K$ most uncertain candidate states and add them to the training pool.

### The Fundamental Flaw of Classical AL in PDEs:
- **Enormous Computational Waste**: Generating $10\times$ solver trajectories requires solving millions of intermediate PDE steps, only to discard $90\%$ of them. This destroys the sample efficiency advantage of active learning.
- **Attractor Confinement**: Every candidate state generated by a numerical solver still relaxes onto the attractor manifold! Classical AL is incapable of proposing true off-manifold boundary states that test and reinforce surrogate stability.

## 💡 The OGAS Philosophy: Operator-Guided Generative Active Sampling

**OGAS** (*Operator-Guided Active Sampling*) solves this fundamental challenge by replacing passive trajectory filtering with **targeted generative state synthesis**.

Instead of waiting for a numerical solver to stumble upon difficult states, **we train a conditional generative model to explicitly synthesize challenging, out-of-attractor physical states, and query the solver for exactly one step.**

## 🔑 Core Innovations of the OGAS Framework

### 1. Optimal Transport Flow Matching (OT-CFM)
Instead of slow, iterative diffusion models (DDPM), OGAS uses Continuous Normalizing Flows trained via **Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching**. This allows straight probability paths between standard Gaussian noise and complex turbulent physical states, generating diverse, high-quality physical states in a fraction of a second.

### 2. Quantile Difficulty Conditioning ($c \in [0, 1]$)
The generator is conditioned on the empirical loss quantile $c = F_\ell(\ell) \in [0, 1]$ of the current surrogate.
- Conditioning on $c \approx 0.2$ generates easy, well-converged attractor states.
- Conditioning on $c \ge 0.8$ directs the generative model to synthesize boundary states where the surrogate is currently weakest, acting as an adaptive automated adversary.

### 3. Multi-Step Pushforward Rollouts
Single-step errors do not always predict multi-step failure. In the Pushforward formulation, the surrogate is unrolled for $H$ steps, and the multi-step horizon error is used to condition the generator. This teaches the generative model to populate the exact seeds of long-term rollout divergence.

### 4. The Stabilizing "Tube" Geometry
By pairing generated off-attractor states $u_{\text{gen}}$ with their exact one-step solver evolution $u_{\text{next}} = \Psi_{\Delta t}(u_{\text{gen}})$, the surrogate learns the **contractive vector field** that pulls perturbed states back toward the attractor. This forms a self-correcting stabilizing tube around the physical manifold.

### 5. 100% Query Efficiency
Every single state generated by the model is advanced by exactly one solver step and added directly to the training pool. There is zero discarded solver compute, achieving unprecedented sample efficiency.
